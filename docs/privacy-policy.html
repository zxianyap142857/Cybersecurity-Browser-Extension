Cybersecurity Browser Extension — Privacy Policy

Applies to the Chrome extension “Cybersecurity Browser Extension” (ID bihhhokkeemejefomgfkhfekibekjclf). Last updated 22 September 2026.

Cybersecurity Browser Extension is a research project from HELP University that detects phishing web pages and malicious links with machine-learning models. It is designed so that your browsing data stays on your device. This page explains exactly what the extension reads, what it stores, and what leaves your computer.

This replaces the version dated 11 April 2026. Changes: tabs and webNavigation permissions removed; webRequest, identity, declarativeNetRequest and privacy added; ad blocking, Privacy Shield, federated learning and on-device inference documented; knowledge-base editing restricted to administrators.

1. What the extension reads

  • The URL and structure of the page you are viewing — only when you run a scan, or when you have switched on automatic scanning in Settings. The page is parsed inside your browser into a compact text representation of its actionable tags (title, forms, inputs, links, buttons, scripts, meta tags) and 43 numeric features.
  • Links and image sources on the page — when the Malicious Link Scanner is on.
  • Top-level response headers — presence and count of security-related headers (for example CSP, HSTS, Set-Cookie). Header values are never stored or transmitted; only yes/no and count signals are derived.
  • Cookies of the current site — only on the Cookies Analyzer page, to display them to you.

2a. Administrator functions

A small number of functions are restricted to administrators who sign in with Google: updating the models, starting federated training, and viewing or editing the chatbot's knowledge base. The sign-in is used only to check the account against a list of administrator e-mail addresses; no Google profile data is requested or stored, and ordinary users never sign in. The chatbot itself remains available to everyone.

Never sent anywhere: raw page HTML, your browsing history, cookie values, header values, screenshots, keystrokes, or any personal identifier. The Ads Remover records nothing at all about which pages you visit or what it blocked; it keeps only a count of hidden elements for the open tab, in memory.

4. Data we (the developers) collect

None. The developers operate no analytics, no crash reporting and no account system for users. The only server-side data are the federated model updates described above, which contain no page content, no URLs and no identity, and which are automatically deleted after 30 days.

5. Third parties

  • Google Cloud hosts the model files and the federated-learning aggregation.
  • Anthropic (api.anthropic.com) receives chat text only if you configure your own API key; their privacy policy applies to that traffic.

We do not sell, share or transfer user data to anyone, and we do not use it for advertising, credit or lending purposes.

6. Your choices

  • Every automatic behaviour (auto page scan, link scanning, blocking, ad removal, Privacy Shield) is off by default and controlled by switches in the popup.
  • The Privacy Shield options (HTTPS upgrade, referrer hiding, tracker blocking, WebRTC protection) are applied by Chrome on your device using packaged rule lists. They do not send traffic through any server and record nothing.
  • “Clear History” on the dashboard deletes stored scan records; removing the extension deletes all extension storage.
  • Reports and local models can be deleted by removing the local agent's data folder.

7. Changes to this policy

If the extension gains a capability that changes what data it reads or sends, this page is updated before that version is published, and the date at the top changes. The Chrome Web Store listing always links to the current version. Material changes are also noted in the extension's release notes on GitHub.
