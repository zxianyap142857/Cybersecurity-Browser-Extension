Cybersecurity Browser Extension — Privacy Policy

Applies to the Chrome extension “Cybersecurity Browser Extension” (ID bihhhokkeemejefomgfkhfekibekjclf). Last updated 26 September 2026.

Cybersecurity Browser Extension is a research project from HELP University that detects phishing web pages and malicious links with machine-learning models. It is designed so that your browsing data stays on your device. This page explains exactly what the extension reads, what it stores, and what leaves your computer.

1. What the extension reads

  • The URL and structure of the page you are viewing — only when you run a scan, or when you have switched on automatic scanning in Settings. The page is parsed inside your browser into a compact text representation of its actionable tags (title, forms, inputs, links, buttons, scripts, meta tags) and 43 numeric features.
  • Links and image sources on the page — when the Malicious Link Scanner is on.
  • Top-level response headers — presence and count of security-related headers (for example CSP, HSTS, Set-Cookie). Header values are never stored or transmitted; only yes/no and count signals are derived.
  • Cookies of the current site — only on the Cookies Analyzer page, to display them to you.

2a. Administrator functions

A small number of functions are restricted to administrators who sign in with Google: reviewing and publishing new model versions, and viewing or editing the chatbot's knowledge base. The sign-in is checked by our model service in Google Cloud, only to compare the account's e-mail address with a list of administrators; no Google profile data is requested or stored, and ordinary users never sign in. Training on your own device is not an administrator function — it is controlled by your own Contribute switch (section 3). The chatbot itself remains available to everyone.

4. Data we (the developers) collect

The developers operate no analytics, no crash reporting and no account system for users. The only data kept on our side are the contributions described in section 3, and only from people who switched contributing on:

DataKept for
Flagged verdicts (three scores and a label)180 days, then deleted automatically
Training updates30 days, then deleted automatically

Each is stored under a random name with only the day it arrived. We do not keep the request logs for these uploads, so your IP address is not stored with them (Google, which runs the servers, necessarily sees it while delivering the request). Because nothing links a contribution to a person, we cannot find or delete an individual's contributions on request; they expire as listed above.

A limitation you should know about. A training update is calculated from the reports on your device. It contains no URLs, but research has shown that a model update can sometimes reveal something about the data it was trained on. To reduce this, a training round requires at least 32 of your reports, and the size of every update is capped before it leaves your device. These measures reduce the risk; they do not remove it entirely. If that matters to you, leave contributing off — detection works the same either way.

5. Third parties

  • Google Cloud hosts our model service: the model files, administrator sign-in, and contributions from users who opted in.
  • Anthropic (api.anthropic.com) receives chat text only if you configure your own API key; their privacy policy applies to that traffic.

We do not sell, share or transfer user data to anyone, and we do not use it for advertising, credit or lending purposes.

6. Your choices

  • Every automatic behaviour (auto page scan, link scanning, blocking, ad removal, Privacy Shield) is off by default and controlled by switches in the popup.
  • Contribute to model improvement (Federated Learning page, or the agent's tray menu) is off by default. Turning it off stops all further contributions immediately; flagged verdicts saved while it is off stay on your computer.
  • The Privacy Shield options (HTTPS upgrade, referrer hiding, tracker blocking, WebRTC protection) are applied by Chrome on your device using packaged rule lists. They do not send traffic through any server and record nothing.
  • “Clear History” on the dashboard deletes stored scan records; removing the extension deletes all extension storage.
  • Reports and local models can be deleted by removing the local agent's data folder.

6a. Known security limitations

We test the extension and its services for security problems and fix what we find. These known limitations are still open; we list them so you can judge them yourself.

  • Another program could impersonate the detection agent while it is not running. The extension talks to the agent at 127.0.0.1:5000 on your computer. While the agent runs, no other program can take that address. If the agent is stopped, a program already running on your computer could listen there and receive the page addresses and page summaries the extension sends for scanning. This needs software already installed on your machine; keep the agent running (or use its start-at-login option) if this concerns you.
  • Websites can tell that the extension is installed. Two small files (a warning icon and a list of ad-hiding rules) must be readable by web pages for the ad and warning features to work, and a site can check for them. This reveals only that the extension is present — nothing about your browsing or settings.
  • Our contribution service does not yet limit how often it can be called. This does not expose anything about you. It means someone could send the service large amounts of junk, which could raise our costs; junk is never used without passing the checks before any new model version is published.
  • Administrator sign-out may take up to 8 hours to apply everywhere. This affects only project administrators, never ordinary users: a signed-out session can remain valid on part of our service until it expires after at most 8 hours.

7. Changes to this policy

If the extension gains a capability that changes what data it reads or sends, this page is updated before that version is published, and the date at the top changes. The Chrome Web Store listing always links to the current version. Material changes are also noted in the extension's release notes on GitHub.
